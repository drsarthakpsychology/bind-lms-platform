# Permission letter — Alexander Street / ProQuest (Counseling and Psychotherapy Transcripts)

Pre-filled example of `publisher-letter-template.md` for the *Counseling and Psychotherapy Transcripts Vols I & II* database, currently published by Alexander Street, now part of ProQuest / Clarivate. This is a subscription product rather than a printed book, so the letter asks for the **institutional tier** (reading access) plus **TDM rights** in one request.

## Why this is the highest-value asset

Of everything in the Casebook corpus, these transcripts matter most to the simulated-patient trainer. They are **real therapy sessions**: how a seasoned clinician actually opens, how silence is handled, how questions are sequenced, how a client discloses and withdraws. From them the application derives the **move patterns** — disclosure precedents, register-matched voice, question sequencing, turn-taking rhythms — that make simulated patients feel like real people rather than scripted actors. Losing them means the trainer teaches from literature and imagination; having them means it teaches from how therapy really sounds. The registry marks this title `priority: 1` for exactly that reason.

[Date]

Alexander Street / ProQuest — Product Enquiries

Dear Alexander Street / ProQuest Team,

I'm writing on behalf of **Casebook**, a training programme in India for early-career counselling and mental-health practitioners. Our students train toward counselling roles — the first people a family often turns to — and they learn by doing: each enrolled student conducts guided interviews with simulated patients in a secure practice application, and their technique is assessed against established clinical practice.

The single most valuable teaching asset for that application is your ***Counseling and Psychotherapy Transcripts, Volumes I & II*** — real sessions, real moves, real patients' ways of speaking. I would like to request **two separate grants of permission**.

### Grant A — institutional subscription (reading access)

We ask to subscribe to the **institutional tier** of *Counseling and Psychotherapy Transcripts Vols I & II*, so that enrolled students of the programme (approximately [N] students, [cohort dates]) can read the transcripts for the duration of their enrolment. Access is limited to enrolled students; nothing is resold, shared, or redistributed. If a trial is available, we would be glad to start there.

### Grant B — text-and-data-mining / AI processing rights

We ask permission to process the transcripts for **one named internal application**: the Casebook simulated-patient practice trainer. Concretely, the processing we request is:

- **Indexing and embedding** the text for semantic retrieval;
- **Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** — to be explicit: RAG counts as AI use, and this request covers it — in which the application retrieves passages to inform its internal reasoning, scoring, and teaching feedback;
- **Derivation of non-substitutive teaching materials** — summaries, rubrics, and reference patterns (notably the move patterns of real therapy sessions) written fresh from what the transcripts teach, which never reproduce the transcript text verbatim beyond short fair-dealing quotation.

We commit that all of this is **non-redistributive**: no verbatim transcript passage is ever served to students or any third party; no copy of the database is shared; the derived materials cannot substitute for the collection. Use is confined to the one named application, access is limited to enrolled students, and we will delete processed copies on request or at the end of the agreement.

We are glad to pay the standard institutional fee plus a fee for Grant B, to report usage if you wish, and to acknowledge Alexander Street / ProQuest in the application's credits.

If any of the above needs adjusting, we are glad to negotiate.
